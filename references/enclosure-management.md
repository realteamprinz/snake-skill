# Enclosure Management Reference

## The Thermal Gradient

### Why It Matters
Snakes are ectotherms — they regulate body temperature by moving between warm and cool areas. A proper thermal gradient is the single most important husbandry factor.

### Setting Up the Gradient
- **Hot side:** Species-specific (typically 85-92°F)
- **Cool side:** Species-specific (typically 72-80°F)
- **Ambient:** Somewhere in between
- Snake should be able to move freely between temperatures
- ALWAYS use a thermostat — unregulated heat sources cause burns and fires

### Heating Methods
- **Under-tank heater (UTH):** Good for belly heat, MUST use thermostat, only heats one spot
- **Radiant heat panel (RHP):** Excellent for PVC enclosures, even heat, thermostat-controlled
- **Ceramic heat emitter (CHE):** No light, good 24/7 heat, use with thermostat
- **Deep heat projector (DHP):** Projects heat deeper than CHE, good for basking
- **Heat tape:** For rack systems, requires thermostat
- **Heat lamp:** Provides light too, daytime use only for most snakes, hot spot risk

### Temperature Monitoring
- Use digital thermometers with probes at hot side, cool side, and ambient
- Check multiple times daily when setting up, then spot-check regularly
- Infrared temp gun for surface temperatures
- Log temperatures especially during season changes (room temp affects enclosure temp)

## Humidity

### Species Requirements
| Species | Normal | During Shed |
|---|---|---|
| Ball Python | 60-80% | 80%+ |
| Corn Snake | 40-50% | 60-70% |
| King Snake | 40-60% | 60-70% |
| Boa Constrictor | 60-70% | 70-80% |
| Hognose | 30-50% | 50-60% |

### Managing Humidity
- Substrate choice matters: coconut fiber and cypress mulch hold humidity, aspen does not
- Restrict ventilation slightly (cover part of screen top) if too dry
- Large water dish on warm side increases ambient humidity
- Misting as needed
- Humid hide (always available, critical during shed)

## Hides

### Why Hides Are Essential
Snakes need to feel secure. Without adequate hides, they are chronically stressed.

### Minimum Setup
- **Warm hide:** On the hot side — snake can be warm AND hidden
- **Cool hide:** On the cool side — snake can cool down AND feel secure
- **Humid hide:** Filled with damp sphagnum moss — critical for shedding

### Hide Quality
- Should be just big enough for the snake to fit snugly (too large doesn't feel secure)
- Multiple entrances can make snakes feel less secure than single-entrance hides
- Dark inside is better than see-through
- Upgrade hide sizes as the snake grows

### Hide Usage as Behavior Indicator
- Always in warm hide = might be trying to fight illness (seeking heat)
- Always in cool hide = hot side might be too hot
- Never using hides = too large, not secure enough, or very confident snake
- Hiding more than usual = stress, pre-shed, illness, or seasonal
- Switching between hides normally = healthy thermoregulation behavior

## Substrate

### Common Options
- **Coconut fiber/coir:** Good humidity retention, natural look, easy to spot-clean
- **Cypress mulch:** Good humidity retention, resists mold, natural
- **Aspen shavings:** Good for dry-climate species, no humidity retention, molds if wet
- **Paper towels/newspaper:** Easy cleaning, good for quarantine or sick snakes, not naturalistic
- **Bioactive substrate:** Advanced — living ecosystem with cleanup crew (isopods, springtails)

### What to Avoid
- Cedar shavings — TOXIC to reptiles (aromatic oils damage respiratory system)
- Pine shavings — controversial, kiln-dried may be safer but many keepers avoid
- Sand alone — impaction risk for most species
- Gravel — impaction risk, no burrowing capability

## Enclosure Types

### Glass Tanks
- Pros: Widely available, good visibility, affordable
- Cons: Poor humidity retention (screen tops), heavy, not ideal for large snakes

### PVC Enclosures
- Pros: Excellent humidity retention, lightweight, stackable, professional look
- Cons: More expensive, less visibility, longer wait (often custom-made)

### Plastic Tubs (Rack Systems)
- Pros: Excellent humidity, space-efficient, easy to clean, snakes feel secure
- Cons: Minimal visibility, less enrichment space, controversial (are they too small?)

### Sizing
- Minimum: The snake should be able to fully stretch out along the length + width
- Many modern keepers advocate for enclosures MUCH larger than old minimums
- More space = more enrichment opportunities = healthier snake
- Upgrade as the snake grows — a baby ball python doesn't need a 4x2x2 but an adult does

## Enrichment

Snakes benefit from environmental enrichment despite the old myth that they "don't need it":
- Climbing branches (even terrestrial species may climb if given the chance)
- Cork bark tubes and flats
- Leaf litter (creates ground cover, encourages natural behaviors)
- Different textures (rough surfaces for shedding)
- Rearranging decor occasionally (new exploration opportunities)
- Outside-the-enclosure handling and exploration time
