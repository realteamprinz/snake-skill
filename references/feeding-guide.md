# Snake Feeding Guide Reference

## Feeding Fundamentals

### Prey Size Rule
- Prey should be approximately 1-1.5x the width of the snake at its widest point
- Too small = not enough nutrition, snake may need multiple prey items
- Too large = risk of regurgitation, stress
- Track prey size relative to snake weight and adjust as they grow

### Frozen-Thawed vs Live
- **Frozen-thawed (F/T):** Recommended. Safer (no risk of prey biting snake), convenient, can stock up
- **Live:** Some snakes refuse F/T and need live prey. NEVER leave live prey unattended with snake — rats can injure or kill snakes
- Transitioning from live to F/T: warm the prey to body temp, wiggle with tongs, try scenting

### Feeding Frequency by Age/Size
- Hatchlings/neonates: Every 5-7 days
- Juveniles: Every 7-10 days
- Sub-adults: Every 10-14 days
- Adults: Every 14-28 days (species-dependent)
- Large boas/pythons (adult): Every 3-4 weeks, sometimes longer

### When NOT to Feed
- During shed cycle (most snakes refuse anyway)
- Within 48 hours of handling
- If the snake regurgitated recently (wait 10-14 days minimum)
- During brumation (temperate species winter cooling)
- If temperatures are wrong (too cold = can't digest, leading to regurgitation)

## Feeding Refusals

### Common Reasons for Refusal
1. **Pre-shed:** Most common reason. Eyes going blue, dull color. Resume feeding after shed
2. **Seasonal fasting:** Ball pythons are notorious — can fast for months in winter. Track weight to ensure they're not losing excessively
3. **Incorrect temperatures:** Too cold to digest = won't eat. Check hot side temps
4. **Stress:** New enclosure, recent move, too much handling, enclosure in high-traffic area
5. **Breeding season:** Males especially may fast during breeding season
6. **Wrong prey type/size:** Try different prey species, different sizes, different presentation
7. **Illness:** If accompanied by other symptoms (wheezing, mouth gaping, lethargy), see a vet

### When to Worry
- Hatchlings: Worry after 2-3 weeks of refusal
- Juveniles: Worry after 4-6 weeks
- Adults: Many species (especially ball pythons) can safely fast for months — track weight
- Rule of thumb: If losing more than 10-15% body weight, consult a vet

## Feeding Response Types

### Calm/Deliberate Striker
- Approaches prey calmly, strikes precisely, wraps and constricts
- Easiest temperament for feeding
- Low risk of accidental bites to handler

### Aggressive Feeder
- Strikes at anything that moves near the enclosure when hungry
- "Food mode" — recognize the signs (S-coil, tracking, tongue flicking rapidly)
- Use long tongs, never hand-feed
- May need to be fed in a separate container (controversial — some say this increases stress)

### Shy/Reluctant Eater
- Won't eat with observer present
- Leave prey item overnight (F/T only — never leave live prey unattended)
- May need privacy, smaller prey, or different prey type
- Reduce handling during non-feeding periods

### Food-Mode Bites vs Defensive Strikes
- **Food-mode:** Snake in S-coil, tracking movement, rapid tongue-flick → sudden wrap-and-constrict strike. They think your hand is prey. Prevention: use tongs, wash hands after handling prey
- **Defensive:** Quick tag-and-release, no constriction. Snake scared or territorial. Prevention: confident handling, hook training

## Regurgitation

A serious event — NOT normal:
- Can indicate: temperature too low, handling too soon after feeding, stress, illness, prey too large
- After regurgitation: Do NOT feed for at minimum 10-14 days
- Next meal should be smaller than usual
- Check all husbandry parameters
- If it happens more than once, see a reptile vet
