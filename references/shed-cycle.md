# Shed Cycle Reference

## The Shed Process

Snakes shed their entire skin in one piece (ideally). This is called ecdysis. The process reveals important information about your snake's health.

## Pre-Shed Signs (In Order)

1. **Dull/faded color** — the first sign, skin looks less vibrant
2. **Blue/opaque eyes** — fluid builds between old and new skin layers, eyes look milky blue (called "being in blue")
3. **Reduced activity** — snake becomes reclusive, stays in hides
4. **Appetite loss** — most snakes refuse food when in shed
5. **Defensive behavior** — can't see well, feels vulnerable, may be more defensive
6. **Eyes clear** — a few days before shedding, eyes clear up (this confuses new owners who think shed is over)
7. **Actual shed** — snake rubs nose on rough surface to start, then crawls out of old skin

Total process: Usually 7-14 days from first signs to completed shed

## Shed Quality Assessment

### Perfect Shed
- One complete piece, inside-out (like taking off a sock)
- Eye caps intact on shed skin
- Tail tip complete
- Indicates: good humidity, healthy snake, proper husbandry

### Incomplete/Piece Shed
- Comes off in multiple pieces
- May indicate: low humidity, dehydration, mites, skin infection
- Action: Increase humidity, provide moist hide, check for underlying issues
- If chronic: reassess entire husbandry setup

### Retained Shed
Critical areas to check:
- **Eye caps (spectacles):** Retained eye caps are serious — can build up layer after layer and cause blindness. Check shed skin for intact eye caps. If missing, the caps are still on the snake
- **Tail tip:** Retained shed on tail can restrict blood flow and cause tip necrosis (loss)
- **Body bands:** Small patches of retained skin usually come off next shed, but assist if near sensitive areas

## Humidity Management During Shed

### Pre-Shed Actions
- Increase humidity by 10-20% above normal
- Add a humid hide (container with damp sphagnum moss, hole for entry)
- Mist the enclosure more frequently
- Ensure water dish is full and large enough for soaking

### Assisting a Difficult Shed
1. Soak the snake in lukewarm water (85°F) for 15-20 minutes
2. After soaking, gently rub with a warm, damp towel
3. NEVER pull retained shed — you can damage new skin underneath
4. For retained eye caps: consult a reptile vet — this is delicate work

## Shed Frequency

### By Age
- Hatchlings/neonates: Every 2-4 weeks (rapid growth)
- Juveniles: Every 4-6 weeks
- Adults: Every 1-3 months
- Seniors: Every 2-4 months

### What Changes in Frequency Mean
- More frequent than expected: Good growth (young), possible health issue (adults), mites (irritant triggers shed)
- Less frequent than expected: Slower growth, cooler temperatures, reduced feeding
- Sudden change in frequency: Investigate — could be environmental or health-related

## Tracking Shed Data

For each shed, log:
- Date
- Quality (one piece / pieces / retained)
- Eye caps present on shed skin (yes/no)
- Tail tip intact (yes/no)
- Days since last shed
- Any retained areas and how resolved
- Humidity levels during pre-shed
- Notes on any concerns
